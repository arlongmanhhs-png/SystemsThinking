# Step 2, paper format

> **Superseded in full, 2 October 2026**, and in part since 28 September. The
> paper form is now a workbook: the workbook pages in `print/src/` and the step
> specifications carry the printed wording and the room each field is given.
> Everything below, the content as well as the arrangement (loose sheets, and
> prompt cards for Step 2), is history rather than instruction, and is kept as
> the record of how the paper form began. The workbook's layout is described in
> `design/workbook.md`. The second pass at the sketch below, with its second
> colour of sticky note, its band of three sentences, and its ten minutes back at
> the sketch sheet, is history too: since 2 October 2026 the second pass is the
> redraw in the book (Exercise 10), the large sheet is the first pass only, and
> one colour of sticky note is enough.

Two sheets, because the step has two halves that want opposite things from a
page. The sketch is a mind dump and wants room, no ruling, and permission to be
messy. The structured work is a dozen actors against a layer, a type, seven
resources, and a dependency, and wants ruled fields. Printing both on one sheet
would make the sketch small and the grid cramped, and it would put the first
thing in the step next to the last.

## The materials

| Item | Format | Per | Reusable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sketch sheet | A1 for a group, A2 for a participant working alone. One side, almost empty | Group or participant, one per case | No: it is drawn on, then photographed and kept |
| Sticky notes, first pass | One colour, square | Table | No |
| Sticky notes, second pass | A second colour, same size | Table | No |
| Structured sheet | A3, printed both sides | Group, the working copy | No |
| Carry-forward sheet | A4, one side | Participant, one per case | No: it is the record that travels |
| Prompt card: actors and the boundary | A5, both sides | Table (one per four or five) | Yes |
| Prompt card: what an actor holds | A5, both sides | Table | Yes |
| Prompt card: authority, responsibility, and accountability | A5, one side | Table | Yes |

Step 2 breaks the Step 1 pattern of an A3 working copy and an A4 neat copy, and
the reason is arithmetic rather than principle. A dozen actors, each with a
layer, a type, seven resources, and a dependency line, does not fit on A4 at a
readable size, so transcribing the grid would be a punishment rather than an
exercise. Instead the A3 stays as the working copy, corrections visible, and the
A4 carries only what the later steps actually read: the boundary statement, the
layers, the allocation of authority, responsibility, and accountability, the two
conflicting descriptions, and the sketch's three sentences. That is the same list
as "what carries forward", which is the test of whether a record is the right
size.

## The sketch sheet

Printed almost empty, because anything printed on the sketch sheet is a
structure, and the whole point of drawing first is that no structure has been
agreed yet. Four things are printed and nothing else.

Across the top, the instruction, in full: **what is going on here? Draw it.**
Underneath it, in smaller type, the three reminders that decide whether a sketch
is usable: use pictures and symbols rather than sentences, write phrases rather
than paragraphs, and show who is in conflict with whom.

In one margin, a key of four marks that may be used, printed as marks rather than
described in words: money, conflict, a blockage, and a question mark for
something the group does not know. Four is deliberate. A longer key becomes a
taxonomy, and a taxonomy is what the structured sheet is for.

Across the foot, a band left blank until the end of the step, with three printed
headings and three lines each: what this system is mainly doing; where the
tension is, meaning who wants what against whom; and what surprised you when you
drew it. The band is printed in a lighter tone so that it reads as closed until
the group comes back to it.

The two colours of sticky note carry the two passes. The first colour goes on at
the start, when nothing has been defined. The second colour goes on at the end,
when the case has layers, actors, and views to arrange the sketch by. Keeping the
two colours apart is what makes the second pass visible: a sheet that is all one
colour has either been drawn once and abandoned, or restructured so thoroughly
that the first thinking has been lost, and both are worth catching. Nothing from
the first pass is removed.

## The structured sheet, front

Seven zones, top to bottom, in the order they are filled. As in Step 1, each zone
is sized to what a good answer needs, which is the quiet way of saying how much
to write.

| Zone | What is printed | Space given |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Inside and outside | Two columns, headed "part of this system" and "next to it, but not part of it". The test is printed between them: would including this change what you would do about the problem? | Eight lines inside, six outside, each outside line with three tick boxes (fixed, out of reach, irrelevant) and room for one line of reason |
| 2. Layers | "What scopes of decision are at work here? Name them, starting with the one whose decisions reach furthest." | Five numbered rows, with a justification line printed against the first and the last only |
| 3. Actor types | "What kinds of actor are there in this case?" with the rule printed: at most five, and no more than one type per three actors | Five short boxes |
| 4. The actors | A grid: actor, layer, type, then the seven resources as tick columns, then one column headed "who needs it from them" | Twelve rows, with a printed note that past roughly fifteen actors either the granularity or the boundary has gone wrong |
| 5. Authority, responsibility, accountability | The decision written out first, then three rows: which layer can take the decision, which is tasked with carrying the decision out, and which answers for the result. "Nobody" printed as an allowed answer | One line for the decision, three rows beneath |
| 6. Competing descriptions | A grid: actor, what that actor takes the problem to be in that actor's own terms, a box for evidenced or inferred, and a column headed "read from what?" | Five rows, with two marked as the minimum that must conflict |
| 7. Who is missing | "Who is not in this list, and why not?" | Three lines |

The dependency column in zone 4 is printed greyed, with a line above the grid
saying which actors it is filled for: those in a layer holding authority,
responsibility, or accountability for the decision in zone 5, and anyone holding
something those actors need. A blank cell there is a state rather than an
omission, which the sheet says in as many words, because a participant looking at
a half-empty column will otherwise fill it.

## The structured sheet, back

Three things, none of which needs writing space, following the same rule as Step
1: the back carries what is read against the finished work, and the reference
material lives on the cards.

1. **The critical check**, printed as a checklist in the order the zones were
   filled, so that each criterion sits opposite the zone it tests. The seven
   criteria are the ones in the Step 2 critical check, unchanged.
2. **The three patterns of authority, responsibility, and accountability**, with
   one line each on what each pattern means for later steps: all three in one
   layer, authority in one layer and responsibility in another, and
   accountability somewhere else or nowhere.
3. **Two worked actor rows**, one good and one wrong, the wrong one showing the
   two failures worth naming: an individual official listed where the department
   that decides is the actor, and an affected group listed as one actor when the
   group splits into two halves that will want different things.

## The prompt cards

**Actors and the boundary** carries what decides who is on the sheet at all. The
four tests, any one of which admits an actor. The two exclusions: a category with
no agency, such as the weather, the market, or society, which is a condition
rather than an actor, and a named individual unless the thing held belongs to
that person rather than to their organisation. The granularity rule, the rule for
splitting an affected group, and the nameable rule. On the reverse, the boundary:
the three inside tests, the three ways out (fixed, out of reach, irrelevant), the
check that asks whether anything left outside could change what the participant
would do, and the note that the boundary stays provisional until the mapping in
Step 5 has run.

**What an actor holds** carries the seven with one line of definition each, and
on the reverse the dependency question, the rule for which actors it is asked of,
and the table of what each resource is used for later. It is the card most likely
to be picked up twice, once when the grid is filled and once when the dependency
column is.

**Authority, responsibility, and accountability** carries the three questions in
the form the sheet asks them, with the places an answer is usually found: a
statute, a mandate, a delegation, or a budget line someone can redirect for
authority; a work programme, a contract, an implementation plan, or a job
description for responsibility; an inspection regime, a reporting duty, an
election, or a complaints procedure for accountability.

## How it runs in class

About 90 minutes for a first pass on the class case, in fixed groups, split three
ways.

Twenty minutes at the sketch sheet, standing, with the sheet flat on a table or
on a wall rather than passed around. Standing matters more than it sounds: a
sheet that sits in front of one person becomes that person's drawing. The
lecturer says nothing in this part beyond the time.

About an hour at the A3, sitting. Two moments are worth structuring, on the same
pattern as Step 1. After zone 1, each group reads its outside column to one other
group, who ask only one question: would including any of these change what you
would do? After zone 6, the same pairing again, this time reading the two
conflicting descriptions aloud and being asked whether both could be acted on at
once. If they could, the pair is not a conflict and the group goes back.

Ten minutes back at the sketch sheet with the second colour, rearranging, and
writing the three sentences in the band at the foot. This is the part most likely
to be cut for time and the part least worth cutting, because the three sentences
are what Step 3 and Step 4 actually read.

Participants then fill their own sheets for their own case in the session set
aside for their own work, where the sketch sheet is A2 rather than A1.

## What is kept

The A4 carry-forward sheet is the record. The A3 and the sketch sheet are
photographed, and the photographs travel with the case, because the
crossings-out on the A3 and the first colour on the sketch are evidence of where
the thinking moved, and Step 5 sends participants back to the boundary often
enough for that to matter. The platform version replaces the sheets once it
exists, but the sheets stay the fallback.

## What the typeset draft changed

Drafted on 28 September and rendered to PDF in `print/`, from the source in
`print/src/`. Two things changed in the drafting, both because the page would not
hold the specification as written.

The dependency question is no longer a greyed column in the actor grid. It is its
own zone on the back of the A3, placed after the allocation of authority,
responsibility, and accountability. The reason is not layout but logic: the set of
actors the dependency question applies to is read off that allocation, so the
column could not be filled at the point the grid reaches it. As its own zone the
dependency also gets room for what the resource is and who needs it for what, which
a tick column never had. The front of the A3 carries the boundary, the layers, the
types, and the actor grid with the seven resources as ticks; the back carries the
allocation, the dependencies, the competing descriptions, who is missing, and the
critical check.

The zones are numbered 1 to 8 rather than 1 to 7, because the dependency is now
counted separately from the resources.

This does not change the method. Decided 28 September: the order in the
description stands, with what each actor holds and who needs it from them as item
6 and the allocation as item 7. The sheet defers the second half of item 6 because
a printed column cannot be filled before the information that decides which cells
to fill exists. A build should follow the description for the order of the steps
and this file for the arrangement of the page, and should not reconcile the two by
renumbering the description.
