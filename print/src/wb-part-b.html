Step 1 · before you write

Define the problem

To fix what is being analysed, what change is wanted, and who you are in relation to it, before any explaining starts.

The problem definition

The problem is written as one sentence in a fixed form. That sentence is called the problem definition throughout this workbook.

There is too much (or too little) of [thing] for [who], since [when], which matters because [consequence].

Then a short rationale saying how and why people are affected. Where the reason it matters is that something is unfair, the rationale says who is affected, compared with whom, and against which expectation. Two questions are worth answering in the rationale: what is at stake, and why do we care? What is driving us to think about this now?

This form does not fit every problem. Three other forms, and when each is needed, are on page .

Eight rules, read against the finished problem definition

  1. One problem per definition. If the sentence needs "and" to hold two things, there are two problems.
  2. Name a thing that can rise or fall. Not "the system is failing", which cannot rise or fall, but "the share of applications decided within the legal term", which can.
  3. No solution in the definition. An action, an instrument, or an organisation that should act is a potential solution: keep it for later, not in the sentence.
  4. No cause in the definition. "Because the ministry underfunds it" is an answer the analysis has to earn, not a premise to start from.
  5. No blame in the definition. Rewrite it so that someone who disagrees about the cause could still accept the sentence as a description.
  6. Bound it. Place, population, and period, so that the questions in Exercise 3 can be answered.
  7. A value can be the reason, not the fact. "This is unfair" is why it matters. The definition itself still needs what there is too much or too little of.
  8. Plain words. If a term would need explaining to someone outside the field, define it in the rationale or drop it.

On neutrality

The problem definition is not value neutral and does not pretend to be. Calling something too little or too much implies an expectation about what the level should be. The rule is that the expectation is named in the rationale rather than hidden in an adjective. "Too few inspections" hides the expectation. "Fewer inspections than the twice-yearly minimum the regulation sets" names it.

Other forms, positions, and size

The check before Exercise 6

Read the problem definition in Exercise 2 against two questions. Could someone who disagrees about the cause still accept the problem definition as a description? Could that person say what would have to change for the problem definition to stop being true? If either answer is no, the problem definition is carrying a cause, a blame, or a solution, and needs to be rewritten in Exercise 6. If both answers are yes, copy the first attempt across to Exercise 6 as it stands. Either way, the agreed problem definition is always written in Exercise 6, because it is the one every later step reads.

When too much and too little does not fit

The form on page is the default, not the only one. Take these in order, and choose one only with a line saying why the default did not fit. All of them carry the same five parts: the thing, the direction or the gap, who it affects, since when, and why it matters.

FormUse it when, and how to write it
MismatchThe quantity is right, but it is in the wrong place, at the wrong time, or with the wrong people. X exists, but not where (or when, or for whom) it is needed: it is at A while the need is at B.
Falling short of a standardThe thing exists and is counted, but fails a rule, a promise, or a quality that was set. X falls short of Y: the standard says A, the practice is B.
Not happened yetA risk, or a capacity that does not exist at all. There is no series to be too high or too low. If A continues, B becomes likely, and nothing currently prevents it.

The five positions, and what each one changes

Your position decides what the analysis ends in, so it is chosen once and carried through.

PositionWhat the analysis ends in
Analysing from outsideFindings. Your own influence is not part of the analysis
Acting for an organisation or a causeA target and an ask. Your own influence is part of the analysis

If the problem is too large, or too small

Exercise 3 is how you know. If you cannot say where the problem starts and stops, or cannot foresee about a dozen things that drive it, it is too large. If almost nothing drives it, it is too small.

Narrow by place, by population, by a single decision, or by period. Widen by asking why the situation persists, or by taking in the decision that sets the conditions for it. The size question in Exercise 3 is a judgement rather than a rule: it asks whether the problem is small enough to work on and still large enough to be worth explaining.

How others describe it, in Exercise 7

Find how the problem is currently described by those with power over it: a policy document, a press release, a minister's answer, an annual report, a minutes entry. Write their description in their words, not yours, and then write what that description takes for granted and what it leaves out. Give the source. One description is enough here; the others come later.

Set the boundary and the perspectives

To decide what is inside the system and what is left out, and whose views count. This is where the case stops being a topic and becomes a system with edges.

Four words this step uses precisely

Actor. Someone who can act in this system or is acted upon by it. Any one of four tests admits an actor: 1 they do something that affects the problem, 2 they are affected by what the system produces, 3 they hold something others in the system need, or 4 they can start or stop a change. Two things are not actors: a category with no agency, such as the weather, the market, or society, which is a condition rather than an actor; and a named individual, unless the thing held belongs to that person rather than to their organisation.

Layer. A set of actors who work within the same scope of decision-making or implementation, meaning that what any of them decides applies to the same territory, population, or part of the organisation. Layers are ordered by how far a decision reaches, from the widest scope of decision to the narrowest, and by nothing else. They are not ranks, not importance, and not a chain of command. They belong to the system, not to you.

Actor type. What kind of actor this is, in this case. The list is written by the participant, at most five types, and as a guide no more than one type per three actors. An actor that seems to need two types usually means the type list is wrong rather than the actor.

Stakeholder is not used in this process. Everything inside the system is an actor.

Drawing the boundary

Almost everything is connected to almost everything else, so "is this related?" admits everything. The test is difference, not relevance: would including this change what the analysis concludes? A candidate goes inside if any one of three things is true. The candidate can increase or decrease the quantity named in the agreed problem definition. The candidate is on the receiving end of the excess or deficit of that quantity, meaning the candidate is part of who the problem is for. Or the candidate sets the rules the first two work under.

Everything left out is marked F fixed, O out of reach, or I irrelevant, with one line of reason. Fixed means it will not change during the period being analysed. Out of reach means it could change but nobody in this case can affect it. Irrelevant means including it would not change what the analysis concludes.

The boundary stays provisional. Everything marked F or O is written down rather than forgotten, because a thing assumed fixed that turns out to move is the commonest reason an intervention fails.

How many actors

About a dozen. Past roughly fifteen, either the granularity or the boundary has gone wrong: individual officials are being listed where the department that decides is the actor, or the boundary has taken in layers where nobody passes any of the three tests above. Check granularity first.

The resources an actor holds

Power, and why this step records resources instead

Power here is an actor's capacity to affect what the system does: what gets decided, what reaches the agenda, and what goes unquestioned. That capacity is hard to judge directly and easy to guess at. What can be recorded instead is concrete: the resources an actor holds that others in the system need. Recording the resources is this step's work; judging what kind of power they add up to comes later, once there is something to judge it against.

The seven resources

Seven kinds, enough to sort by and few enough to remember.

ResourceWhat it means
Formal authorityCan decide, or change a rule
MoneyHolds or can redirect the funding
Delivery capacityDoes the work on the ground
Information and expertiseKnows something the others need to know
AccessCan get to whoever decides
LegitimacyCan credibly speak for others
Public attentionCan make an issue visible, or keep it quiet

Two rules while you fill the table

  1. An actor that seems to need two layers is usually two actors, as a ministry's policy directorate and its inspectorate are two actors with different reach.
  2. An affected group that splits into halves wanting different things is two actors. A single entry that hides the split will not survive the rest of the analysis.

A third rule, about who gets left out

Include those the system acts on, not only those who act. Name the group by its position in the system ("households refused on income grounds since 2023").

The four columns before the resources

Columns 1 to 4 are the four admission tests from page : 1 affects the problem, 2 is affected by what the system produces, 3 holds something others need, 4 can start or stop a change. Tick every one that applies. An actor with no tick in any of the four does not belong in the table, and column 2 is the one to read back: if nothing is ticked down that column, the list has only the actors who act, and not those the system acts on.
