| 2 Oct 2026 | **Where a case is kept, reworked after review (B02).** Two reviewers found that the browser copy undid the privacy the account gives (the next person on a shared computer read the previous person's case from the browser), and that the account's case replaced newer work (a write cut short by closing the tab, a failed write, or typing before the account had answered). Now the browser holds two kinds of slot: the browser-only slot, for whoever uses the page without an account, and one slot per signed-in person, named by the account id, with a record beside it saying whether the copy holds a change the account has not confirmed and which case of the account's the copy descends from. The first time a person signs in on a browser, the browser-only copy becomes that person's and is carried into an empty area; where the account already holds a case, the browser-only copy stays where it is. The database layout now names every document in the head and never rewrites a part once written, so a write cut short leaves the previous case whole and a torn read only ever means a newer head is on its way: the page reads again, up to four times, then says the case could not be read, with "Read the case again", and stays read only until a read succeeds; the footer's three buttons wait the same way. The views see nothing of this beyond the read-only state and the footer.
