# ADVO4: this course's settings

Everything here is true of one course and of nothing else. Nothing in this file
belongs in `core/` or in `steps/`.

**The course.** Advocacy: Influencing Multi-Level Governance (Osiris
IPPL-FT2ADVO4-26), year 2 of the International Public Policy and Leadership
programme at The Hague University of Applied Sciences. Teaching runs from
16 November 2026 to 5 February 2027.

**The position it teaches.** Acting for an organisation or a cause. Step 12
produces a target actor, a level of governance, an arena, an instrument, a moment
in the policy cycle, and an ask.

**Critical checks.** Self-assessed. No lecturer signs off a step. This is a
setting-level decision and not a property of the process.

**Topics.** Participants choose their own case and need no approval for it.
Criteria replace approval. Decided 28 September 2026: they are written as **one
combined list in this manual**, from which the platform takes the five that are
method (the Step 1 suitability check), word for word. The reasoning is that the
tool has to stand on its own, because there will not always be a course guide,
while this manual may add what is course policy rather than method. The manual
may extend the list; it may never contradict the five.

Decided 28 September 2026: **one list for group and individual topics**, and a
**purely organisational challenge is allowed**, with no governance layer
required. The list follows.

### The topic criteria

The first five are the method's, and are the Step 1 suitability check word for
word. They are the same in the manual and in the platform, and neither may be
changed without the other.

**To be checked against the method before teaching starts on 16 November 2026**
(decided 2 October 2026). The method has moved since this list was written: four
of the five questions below no longer match page 9 of the workbook word for word,
and page 9 prints the procedure question apart, as the one question that
disqualifies a topic. The check covers the whole manual, the class-case line
below included, and a second, shorter check at the end of the work catches what
Phases B to E change.

1. Has this been going on rather than happening once?
2. Does it have a history you can describe?
3. Have attempts to deal with it failed?
4. Could a known procedure solve it if someone chose to? (If yes, it is not a
   case for this process.)
5. Is it small enough to work on: can you say where it starts and stops, and
   could you describe what drives it in about a dozen variables?

The rest is course policy rather than method, and applies in ADVO4 only.

6. The case is yours to choose, and needs no approval. These criteria replace
   approval, and you answer them yourself in writing in Step 1.
7. A social challenge or an organisational one both qualify. A governance layer
   is not required, though a case with more than one layer gives Step 2 more to
   work with.
8. The case runs for the whole block. Choosing a case you can drop is the most
   expensive mistake available here.
9. You can reach enough about it to answer Step 2 and Step 3: who the actors are,
   and what the pattern over time has been.

Nothing further has been set. If the programme requires anything else of a case
(ethics, data access, the language of sources, or cases already in use in another
course), it is added here and nowhere else.

**Assessment.** Led by the lecturer, not by the tool. Step 8 (weigh consequences
and interests) carries MLO 2003.

**What a lecturer reads of a case** (decided 2 October 2026). Only what the
student hands in, through the course's own hand-in: the saved case file, or the
file of the whole of Phase A the platform offers at the end of the phase. A
hand-in is not a sign-off. What is handed in, and when, is set with the
milestones, still to be written. A lecturer view in the platform comes in a later
version.

**Ethical frameworks and professional codes** (decided 2 October 2026). Step 8
asks the participant to justify the choice against stated values, and the method
defines neither "relevant ethical frameworks" nor "professional codes". Which
ethical frameworks and professional codes ADVO4 expects is set here, for the
course as a whole, as a grading question, with the assessment rubric. Not yet
written.

**The class case.** Worked in fixed groups on the A3 framing sheet, then
transcribed onto A4. Participants work their own case in the session of the week
set aside for their own work.

Still to be written here: the session structure, the learning goals mapping, the
milestones, and the assessment rubric. How long each session gives a step is
decided when the sessions are designed. No time is set for Step 2 in class: the
90 minutes suggested for Step 2's first pass on the class case is withdrawn
(Ashley, 2 October 2026).
